Should a Self-Driving Car Choose Who to Save?

A self-driving car's AI has to make split-second decisions. But what happens when every option causes harm? Today we're going to think like the engineers, ethicists, and lawmakers who actually have to answer this.

There's no single correct answer in this lesson — the goal is to build the best reasoning you can.

The dilemma

Picture this

A self-driving car is about to crash. It has two choices, and it must pick one:

🅰️ Protect the one passenger inside the car.
🅱️ Swerve to avoid the passenger, but harm three pedestrians instead.

There's no time to stop. No third option. What should the AI do — and why?

Responsibility / Trust

"The person bought the car and trusted it to keep them safe."

Role-based reasoning

"The AI is part of the car — its first job is protecting the person inside."

Consequence-based

"If one person can be saved instead of three, save three."

Fairness

"The AI shouldn't give special treatment to the car's owner."

Problem-solving

"Look for another option instead of choosing who gets hurt."

Creative thinking

"Maybe it should brake, or steer somewhere fewer people are at risk."

Curveballs

Two tricky answers

Student says: "The AI should not decide who lives or dies."

If the accident is unavoidable and the AI must choose an action — what rule should it follow? (This moves them from refusing to choose, to actually reasoning.)

Student says: "It depends on who caused the accident."

Should the AI punish the person who caused the accident, or just try to prevent harm? (This introduces justice vs. safety.)
